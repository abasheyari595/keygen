Radiological Anatomy - Basic Views

Radiological Anatomy Positioning Guide

Upper Respiratory Airway

Pharynx (Lateral View)

  • Positioning: Patient is standing or sitting in a lateral position. The shoulder touches the standing bucky. Neck should be extended.
  • Central Ray (CR): Perpendicular (90°) to the angle of the mandible.
    • FFD (Focal-Film Distance): 150 cm to compensate for the distance.
    • Collimation: From skull base to lower neck, and from nose to behind the cervical spine.

Larynx (Lateral View)

  • Positioning: Same as the pharynx view.
  • Central Ray (CR): Perpendicular (90°) to the laryngeal cartilage.
    • Collimation: From the nose to the thoracic inlet, and from the anterior skin of the larynx to behind the cervical spine.

Chest X-Ray

1. Chest X-Ray (Postero-Anterior - PA View)

  • Positioning: Patient stands facing the bucky (chest against it).
    • Hands are placed on the lower back/buttocks.
    • Shoulders are pushed forward to move the scapulae away from the lung fields.
    • Chin is raised above the bucky.
    • Cassette top should be 2 cm above the shoulders.
  • Central Ray (CR): Centered on the bucky, aligned with the mid-sagittal plane at the level of the fifth dorsal vertebra (T5). Patient should hold their breath after a deep inspiration.
  • Collimation: Include lower neck, diaphragm, apices, and chest walls.

Chest X-Ray (Continued)

2. Chest X-Ray (Lateral View)

  • Positioning: Patient stands with their side against the bucky.
    • Arms are raised over the head.
    • Cassette is 2 cm above the shoulder.
    • Left Lateral view is standard for heart, left lung, or both lungs diseases.
  • Central Ray (CR): Centered on the bucky, aligned with the mid-axillary line.

3. Chest X-Ray (Antero-Posterior - AP View)

  • Positioning: Patient stands with their back against the bucky.
    • Hands on waist to move scapulae away from the lung fields.
  • Central Ray (CR): Over the angle of the sternum.
  • Positioning:
    • AP Cephalic Angle: AP position with a 30° cephalic (upward) tube angle. CR is below the clavicle.
    • PA Caudal Angle: PA position with a 30° caudal (downward) tube angle. CR is at the lung apex.
  • Positioning: AP position where the patient leans back significantly, so the shoulders touch the bucky while their body is forward. CR is horizontal to the mid-sternum.
  • Positioning: Patient lies on their side (e.g., left lateral decubitus means the left side is down).
    • For suspected pleural effusion, the affected side should be down.

7. Chest for Intensive Care & Old Patients

  • Positioning: Typically done as a recumbent (supine) or semi-sitting AP view if the patient cannot stand. A lateral view can be performed with the cassette placed at the patient's side (cross-table lateral).
  • Positioning: Can be done standing (PA) with support, or more commonly, as a supine AP view with both hands raised above the head.

Thoracic Cage

Sternum

  • PA View with Angle: Patient is prone. CR is 10 cm paramidline at axilla level, angled medially towards the sternum.
  • Oblique PA View: Patient is prone, one side raised 30°. CR is 10 cm paramidline on the raised side.
  • Lateral View: Patient stands in a lateral position with shoulders pulled back. CR is directed to the middle of the sternum.

The Ribs (1st to 10th)

  • Antero-Posterior (AP) View:
    • Positioning: Patient stands with back against the cassette.
    • CR: On the sternal angle.
  • Postero-Anterior (PA) View:
    • Positioning: Patient stands facing the cassette.
    • CR: Over the 4th dorsal vertebra (T4).
  • Oblique View:
    • Positioning: Patient rotated 45° from AP, with the examined side touching the cassette.

The Ribs (9th to 12th)

  • AP View:
    • Positioning: Patient supine or standing AP. Cassette extends from nipple to iliac crest level.
    • CR: Center of the cassette.

Sternoclavicular Joint

  • Straight PA View: CR at D1/D2 level.
  • Oblique View: Patient rotated 20° from PA for each side (for comparison). CR is vertical over the raised side at D1/D2.

Shoulder

Basic Views

  • 1. AP View:
    • Positioning: Patient stands with the diseased shoulder against the cassette (rotated 15-30°). Arm is supinated and slightly abducted.
    • CR: On the coracoid process.
    • Respiration: Arrested (hold breath) to avoid motion.
  • 2. Axial View:
    • Supero-inferior: Patient sits, arm abducted 45°. CR is vertical from superior to inferior, aimed at the humeral head.
    • Infero-superior: Patient is supine, arm abducted. Cassette is on top of the shoulder. CR is horizontal into the axilla.
  • 1. Outlet View:
    • Positioning: Patient in a lateral position relative to the cassette, rotated forward. Arm extended backward.
    • CR: Posteriorly over the shoulder with a 10-15° caudal angulation.
  • 2. Y-View (Scapular Lateral Oblique):
    • Positioning: Patient stands PA, rotated 45-60° with the affected shoulder against the cassette.
    • CR: To the medial border of the scapula.

Clavicle

Basic Views

  • 1. AP View:
    • Positioning: Patient supine or standing AP. In children, both sides are done for comparison.
    • CR: 90° to the mid-clavicle.
  • 2. PA View:
    • Positioning: Patient sitting or standing PA, slightly rotated to bring the clavicle in contact with the cassette. Head turned to the other side.
    • CR: To the mid-clavicle.

Humerus

Views

  • 1. AP View:
    • Positioning: Patient sits/stands/lies with the back of the humerus on the cassette. Arm slightly abducted, humeral condyles parallel to the table.
    • CR: 90° to the mid-humeral shaft.
  • 2. Lateral View:
    • Positioning: Arm internally rotated, elbow flexed to 90°, hand on the lower abdomen.
    • CR: On the mid-humeral shaft.
  • Transthoracic Lateral Projection:
    • Positioning: Patient stands in a lateral position, with the uninjured arm raised over their head.
    • CR: Directed horizontally through the chest (thorax) to the surgical neck of the affected humerus.

Elbow

Basic Views

  • 1. AP View:
    • Positioning: Patient sits with the entire arm at the same level. Forearm is fully extended and hand is supinated.
    • CR: On the elbow joint, 2 cm below the humeral epicondyles.
  • 2. Lateral View:
    • Positioning: Elbow flexed at 90°. Hand is in a lateral position (thumb up).
  • 1. Axial View (Acute Flexion):
    • Positioning: Two separate images are taken: one with the forearm parallel to the cassette, and one with the humerus parallel to the cassette.
  • 2. Lateral with Oblique X-ray (for Radial Head):
    • Positioning: Standard lateral position.
    • CR: To the radial head with the beam angled 45° towards the body.

Forearm

  • AP View: Hand supinated. Must include both wrist and elbow joints.
  • Lateral View: Elbow flexed 90°, hand in lateral position. Must also include both joints.
  • CR (for both): 90° to the middle of the forearm.

Wrist

Basic Views

  • 1. PA View:
    • Positioning: Patient sits, forearm resting on the table, palm down (prone).
    • CR: 90° on the dorsum of the wrist, between the radial and ulnar styloid processes.
  • 2. Lateral View:
    • Positioning: From PA view, rotate hand and wrist 90° so the ulnar side is down.
    • CR: On the radial styloid process.
  • 1. PA with Ulnar Deviation (Scaphoid View):
    • Positioning: From PA view, gently deviate the wrist towards the ulna (little finger side).
    • CR: 90° on the scaphoid, often with a 20-30° angle towards the forearm.
  • 2. PA Oblique View:
    • Positioning: Wrist rotated 45° with the ulnar side in contact with the cassette.
  • 3. Axial View (Carpal Tunnel View):
    • Positioning: Hand is hyperextended back.
    • CR: Angled 25° towards the forearm, falling on the wrist.

Hand and Fingers

Hand - Basic Views

  • PA View: Palm of the hand flat on the cassette, fingers slightly spread. CR on the 3rd metacarpal head.
  • Lateral View: Ulnar side of the hand is on the cassette (like a "karate chop"). Used for locating foreign bodies, not for fracture diagnosis due to overlapping bones.
  • PA Oblique View: Hand rotated 45°, ulnar side down. Fingers are parallel to the cassette. This view "opens up" the joints.
  • Fan Lateral View: Hand in lateral position, but fingers are fanned out and separated. CR on the 2nd metacarpal head.

Fingers

  • Views: PA, Lateral, and Oblique views are taken for the specific finger of interest.
  • CR (for all views): 90° over the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint of the desired finger.

Thumb

  • AP View: Requires internal rotation of the hand to place the thumb's dorsal aspect on the cassette.
  • PA View: Hand is in a lateral position to get a PA of the thumb.
  • Lateral View: Hand is in a PA position (palm down) to get a lateral of the thumb.
  • CR (for all views): 90° over the interphalangeal joint.

Pelvis & Both Hips

Basic Views

  • 1. AP View (Both Hips):
    • Positioning: Patient is supine.
      • Key step: Internally rotate both feet 15-20° to overcome femoral neck anteversion and properly visualize the femoral necks.
      • Heels should be 20-25 cm apart.
    • CR: In the midline, 5 cm above the symphysis pubis.
    • Respiration: Suspend breath in expiration.
  • 2. AP View (Single Hip):
    • Positioning: Same as for both hips.
    • CR: 6 cm below the midpoint of a line between the ASIS (Anterior Superior Iliac Spine) and the symphysis pubis.
  • 3. Lateral Oblique (Frog-Leg Position):
    • Positioning: Patient is supine. The hip and knee of the affected side are flexed and abducted (like a frog's leg). The other side is elevated.
    • CR: At the skin crease in the inguinal region.
  • True Lateral (Cross-Table Lateral):
    • Positioning: Patient is supine. Cassette is placed vertically against the affected hip. The other leg is lifted out of the way.
    • CR: Horizontal beam aimed medio-laterally.
  • Von Rosen Position:
    • Positioning: Child is supine, with both legs abducted 45° each (total 90° abduction).

Sacroiliac Joints, Sacrum & Coccyx

Sacroiliac (SI) Joints

  • PA View: Patient prone. CR between both SI joints with a 10° caudal angle.
  • AP View with Cephalic Angulation: Patient supine.
    • CR: 5 cm above the symphysis pubis, with a 25° cephalic angulation.
  • Oblique View: Patient supine, rotated 20-25°. The side being examined is elevated.
    • CR: On the raised side, 5 cm medial to the ASIS.

Sacrum & Coccyx

  • AP View:
    • Positioning: Patient supine with legs flexed.
    • CR (Sacrum): 5 cm above symphysis pubis with a 15° cephalic angle.
    • CR (Coccyx): 5 cm above symphysis pubis with a 10° caudal angle.
  • Lateral View:
    • Positioning: Patient in a true lateral position with legs flexed to maintain stability.
    • CR (Sacrum): 10 cm posterior to the ASIS.
    • CR (Coccyx): Directly over the coccyx.

Femur and Knee

Femur

  • AP View: Patient supine with leg extended and internally rotated 15°. CR in the middle of the cassette.
  • Lateral View: Patient lies on the side being examined, with the knee flexed 45°. CR in the middle of the cassette.

Knee - Basic Views

  • AP View: Patient supine with leg straight.
    • CR: 1 cm below the lower pole of the patella. Angle varies: thin patient (5° caudal), average (90°), obese patient (5° cephalic).
  • Lateral View: Patient lies on the side to be examined, with the knee flexed 30°.
    • CR: To the anterior part of the femoral condyle.
  • Axial / Tangential / Sunrise View:
    • Positioning: Can be done supine (knee flexed 90°, cassette held by patient) or prone (knee flexed 90°, CR angled caudally).
  • Tunnel View:
    • Positioning: Patient is prone with knee flexed 40-50°.
    • CR: Directed into the knee joint with a 40-50° caudal angle.

Leg (Tibia & Fibula) and Ankle

Leg (Tibia & Fibula)

  • AP View: Patient supine, leg extended and slightly rotated medially.
  • Lateral View: Patient lies on the side to be examined, with the lateral side of the leg on the cassette.
  • CR (for both): 90° to the mid-portion of the leg.

Ankle - Basic Views

  • AP View: Patient supine, knee extended, foot flexed 90° (dorsiflexed).
  • Lateral View: Patient sitting, ankle rotated with the lateral aspect on the cassette. Foot dorsiflexed to 90°.
  • CR (for both): On the ankle joint (mid-distance between malleoli for AP, on medial malleolus for lateral).
  • Oblique View: From AP, ankle is rotated medially 45°.
  • Mortise View:
    • Positioning: As AP, but with the ankle rotated 15-20° medially.
  • Forced Inversion View (Stress View):
    • Positioning: As AP, but the examiner pushes the foot medially (inversion).

Heel and Foot

Heel (Calcaneus)

  • Lateral View: Patient lies on their side, with the lateral aspect of the foot on the cassette. Foot is dorsiflexed 90°.
    • CR: 90° on the upper part of the calcaneus.
  • Axial View: Patient sits with knee extended. Foot is dorsiflexed (toes pulled back with gauze).
    • CR: Angled 30° cephalic (towards the body) into the plantar surface of the heel.

Foot

  • AP View (Dorsi-Plantar): Patient sits with knee flexed, and the plantar surface (sole) of the foot is flat on the cassette.
    • CR: 90° to the tarsal bones.
  • AP Oblique View: From AP, the foot is rotated to the medial aspect.
  • Lateral View: Patient lies on their side, with the foot's lateral aspect on the cassette.
  • Lateral View (Weight-Bearing):
    • Positioning: Patient stands on a vertical cassette placed between the feet.

Forefoot (Toes)

  • Views include AP (Dorsi-Plantar), Lateral, and Oblique for the entire forefoot or individual toes. The big toe often requires its own specific views (AP, Oblique, Lateral).

Cervical Spine

Basic Views

  • AP View: Patient stands/sits/supine facing the tube. Chin is pushed forward and upward.
    • CR: At C4 (epiglottis level) with a 15-20° cephalic angulation.
  • Lateral View: Patient in lateral position, shoulders dropped down to visualize C7. Chin moved forward.
    • CR: Horizontal, at C3-C4 level.
  • Open Mouth View:
    • Positioning: Patient supine, mouth wide open.
    • CR: Vertically through the open mouth.
  • Oblique View:
    • Positioning: From AP, patient is rotated 45°.
    • CR: To C4, with a 15° cephalic angle.
  • Dynamic Views (Flexion & Extension):
    • Positioning: Done in the lateral view, with the patient flexing and extending their neck as far as possible.

Dorsal (Thoracic) Spine

  • AP View: Patient is supine with legs flexed. Film is exposed after deep inspiration to improve contrast.
    • CR: To the cassette center at D7 (mid-sternum).
  • Lateral View: Patient in lateral position, arms above the head.
    • CR: Through the axilla at D6 level.
  • Oblique View: Patient rotated 45°. Arms elevated.
    • CR: On the mid-clavicular line of the elevated side.

Cervico-Dorsal Junction (C4-D5)

  • AP View: Patient supine or standing. CR at the suprasternal notch.
  • Lateral View (Swimmer's View):
    • Positioning: Patient in lateral view. The arm closer to the cassette is raised above the head, while the other arm is depressed. This separates the shoulders.
    • CR: Through the axilla close to the film.

Dorso-Lumbar Junction

  • Positioning: AP and Lateral views. Exposure is taken at the end of expiration.
  • CR (AP): Over the xiphoid process.
  • CR (Lateral): On the spine at D12.

Lumbosacral Spine

Basic Views

  • AP View: Patient supine with hips and knees flexed to make the lumbar spine flat on the table.
    • CR: In the midline at a level 3 cm above the iliac crest.
  • Lateral View: Patient in lateral position, with a pad under the waist to prevent spine sagging.
    • CR: In the mid-axillary line, 3 cm above the iliac crest.
  • Oblique Views:
    • Positioning: Patient rotated 45°.
    • CR: 3 cm above the raised iliac crest at its mid-clavicular line.
  • Dynamic Views (Flexion & Extension):
    • Positioning: Patient stands in a lateral position, bending forward (flexion) and backward (extension).

Radiography for Scoliosis

  • Views Taken:
    • AP Dorso-lumbar spine (standing) is the primary view.
    • AP with bending to the right side.
    • AP with bending to the left side.
    • A lateral view is also taken.
