1–2) Bone Structure & Types

Long bone: Epiphysis (ends, covered by articular cartilage), Diaphysis (shaft with medullary cavity), Metaphysis (with epiphyseal plate in growing bones). Outer compact bone provides strength; inner spongy bone (trabeculae) contains red or yellow marrow.

Shapes: Long (femur), Short (tarsals), Flat (scapula), Irregular (vertebrae), Sesamoid (patella).

6) Terms of Movement

Movements are described in anatomical position:

  • Flexion / Extension: decrease / increase angle at joint.
  • Abduction / Adduction: away from / toward midline.
  • Rotation: movement around long axis (medial / lateral).
  • Circumduction: circular movement combining flexion, abduction, extension, adduction.
  • Supination / Pronation: palm up / down.
  • Inversion / Eversion: sole inward / outward.
  • Elevation / Depression: raise / lower a part.
  • Protraction / Retraction: forward / backward (e.g. jaw).
  • Opposition: thumb touches fingers.

7–10) Shoulder Girdle & Clavicle

Shoulder girdle: connects upper limb to trunk. Only true joint with axial skeleton is the sternoclavicular joint. Provides wide mobility but less stability.

Clavicle: S-shaped bone. Sternal end articulates with sternum, acromial end with scapula. Functions to transmit weight from limb to axial skeleton, and keeps limb away from trunk.

Dr. Abdelrahman

11) Lower Limb Overview & Foot

Role: weight-bearing and locomotion; bones are larger and joints more stable than upper limb.

Regions: Thigh (femur), Leg (tibia & fibula), Foot (tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges).

Foot bones: 7 Tarsals — Talus (ankle bone, transmits weight), Calcaneus (heel), Navicular, Cuboid, 3 Cuneiforms. 5 Metatarsals (arch framework), 14 Phalanges (toes; hallux has 2, others 3).

Arches: medial & lateral longitudinal, transverse — function as shock absorbers and weight distributors.

Ankle joint: Talocrural, hinge type. Formed by tibia & fibula malleoli gripping talus (mortise).

Dr. Abdelrahman

12) Leg — Tibia & Fibula

Tibia: medial, weight‑bearing. Proximal condyles articulate with femur; tibial tuberosity for patellar ligament. Distal medial malleolus forms medial ankle.

Fibula: lateral, slender, non–weight bearing. Head articulates with tibia, distal lateral malleolus forms outer ankle, stabilizes ankle joint.

Dr. Abdelrahman

13) Knee Joint & Patella

Knee: modified hinge synovial joint between femoral condyles, tibial condyles, and patella. Allows flexion, extension, slight rotation.

  • Menisci: medial & lateral fibrocartilages, improve congruence, absorb shock.
  • Ligaments:
    • MCL & LCL prevent side deviation.
    • ACL prevents anterior tibial displacement; PCL prevents posterior displacement.
    • Patellar ligament continues quadriceps tendon to tibial tuberosity.
  • Patella: largest sesamoid, within quadriceps tendon; improves leverage for knee extension.
Dr. Abdelrahman

14) Thigh — Femur & Hip Joint

Femur: longest bone. Head articulates with acetabulum; neck angled ~125° (fracture-prone). Greater & lesser trochanters for muscle attachment. Shaft slightly anterior convexity. Distal condyles articulate with tibia; intercondylar fossa between them.

Hip joint: ball-and-socket; stable due to deep acetabulum + strong ligaments (iliofemoral prevents hyperextension, pubofemoral prevents over-abduction, ischiofemoral reinforces posteriorly).

Dr. Abdelrahman

15) Pelvis & Hip Bone

Pelvis: basin‑like ring of hip bones + sacrum. Transmits body weight to lower limbs, protects pelvic organs, provides muscle attachments.

Hip bone: fusion of ilium, ischium, pubis at acetabulum. - Ilium: iliac crest, articulates with sacrum at sacroiliac joint.
- Ischium: ischial tuberosity (“sitting bone”).
- Pubis: pubic symphysis; with ischium forms obturator foramen.
