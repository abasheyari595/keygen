1–2) Bone Structure & Types

Long bone: Epiphysis (articular cartilage), Diaphysis (shaft, medullary cavity), Metaphysis with epiphyseal plate. Compact bone = strong outer shell; Spongy bone = trabeculae, marrow spaces.

Shapes: Long (femur), Short (tarsals), Flat (scapula), Irregular (vertebra), Sesamoid (patella).

Dr. Abdelrahman

3) Bone Coverings

Periosteum: outer fibrous layer + inner osteogenic layer; covers bone except at articular cartilage. Rich blood supply & nerves; essential for growth, repair, nutrition.

Endosteum: thin membrane lining medullary cavity and trabeculae; contains osteoblasts & osteoclasts.

Dr. Abdelrahman

4) Bone Marrow

Red marrow: hematopoietic (blood formation), found in flat bones & epiphyses of long bones in adults.

Yellow marrow: mainly fat; in medullary cavity of long bone diaphysis; can convert to red if severe blood loss.

Dr. Abdelrahman

5) Ossification

Intramembranous ossification: bone develops directly from mesenchyme (e.g. flat bones of skull).

Endochondral ossification: bone replaces a cartilage model (e.g. long bones). Primary ossification center in diaphysis; secondary centers in epiphyses.

Dr. Abdelrahman

6) Joints & Movements

Types of joints: - Fibrous (immovable, e.g. sutures).
- Cartilaginous (slightly movable, e.g. intervertebral discs).
- Synovial (freely movable, most limb joints). Components: capsule, synovial membrane, cavity, articular cartilage.

Movements:

  • Flexion / Extension; Abduction / Adduction; Rotation; Circumduction.
  • Supination / Pronation; Inversion / Eversion; Elevation / Depression.
  • Protraction / Retraction; Opposition (thumb).
Dr. Abdelrahman

7–10) Shoulder Girdle & Clavicle

Shoulder girdle: scapula + clavicle; only bony link to trunk is sternoclavicular joint. Provides wide range of motion but less stability.

Clavicle: S-shaped; medial end with sternum, lateral with acromion. Transmits limb weight, keeps limb away from trunk.

Dr. Abdelrahman

11) Lower Limb Overview & Foot

Function: support, locomotion. Regions: thigh, leg, foot.

Foot bones: 7 tarsals (talus, calcaneus, navicular, cuboid, 3 cuneiforms), 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges. Arches: medial, lateral longitudinal, transverse — act as shock absorbers.

Ankle joint: hinge; tibia & fibula mortise grips talus for stability.

Dr. Abdelrahman

12) Leg — Tibia & Fibula

Tibia: medial, weight-bearing; condyles articulate with femur; tibial tuberosity for patellar ligament; distal medial malleolus forms ankle.

Fibula: slender, lateral, not weight-bearing; head with tibia; distal lateral malleolus forms outer ankle and stabilizes joint.

Dr. Abdelrahman

13) Knee Joint & Patella

Knee: modified hinge; bones: femoral condyles, tibial condyles, patella.

  • Menisci: medial & lateral fibrocartilage; shock absorption, congruence.
  • Ligaments: MCL, LCL (side); ACL, PCL (AP stability); patellar ligament.
  • Patella: largest sesamoid, in quadriceps tendon; increases leverage for extension.
Dr. Abdelrahman

14) Thigh — Femur & Hip Joint

Femur: head into acetabulum; neck (125° angle); greater & lesser trochanters; shaft curved; distal condyles form knee.

Hip joint: ball-and-socket; reinforced by strong ligaments (iliofemoral, pubofemoral, ischiofemoral).

Dr. Abdelrahman

15) Pelvis & Hip Bone

Pelvis: hip bones + sacrum; transmits weight, supports viscera, muscle attachment.

Hip bone: fusion of ilium (iliac crest, sacroiliac joint), ischium (ischial tuberosity), pubis (pubic symphysis, obturator foramen). Meet at acetabulum.
